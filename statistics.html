Read the archive carefully

Patterns in this dataset—not population estimates.

The observations come mainly from voluntarily donated 23andMe DNA Relatives exports, supplemented by a small number of documented MAGENHA lineages. They form a convenience sample with especially strong Cuban representation.

Percentages below describe only this archive. They should not be treated as national prevalence, a representative survey, or evidence of ancestry for any individual.

For privacy, country/type breakdowns require at least 30 observations and displayed cells require at least 5. Additional cells may be withheld to prevent rare counts being recovered by subtraction, so a blank can mean zero or withheld and visible rows may not total 100%.

Method & limits

What the percentages mean.

01

Country profiles

Each published Y-DNA share is divided by that country’s Y-DNA observation count. Each published mtDNA share is divided separately by that country’s mtDNA count.

02

Distribution map

For the chosen lineage, a country’s published matching count is divided only by observations of the same DNA type in that country. The minimum-observation control applies to that denominator.

03

Disclosure controls

The public file contains aggregate counts only. It omits country/type sets below 30 observations, cells below 5, and complementary cells needed to stop rare values being reconstructed from totals. It includes no names, profile links, raw DNA, match lists, or testing-account information.

04

Interpretation

Sampling is voluntary and non-random. Relatedness, geographic imbalance, reporting patterns, and small samples can all affect the percentages.

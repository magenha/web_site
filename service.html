How we work

A research case built around your goals.

MAGENHA offers historical and genealogical documentary research in Cuba through its network of authorized local collaborators. Research may include Catholic parish and diocesan baptism, marriage and burial records, historical Civil Registry records where access and collaborators are available, and documentary reconstruction and verification of historical family relationships.

We reconstruct family lines, review and verify previous genealogies, analyze the information already available, and identify relevant ancestors, locations, parishes, and archives. We work especially with sources that may be difficult to access or unavailable online.

Availability is evaluated case by case and depends on the locality, period, archive, survival of the records and current access. Historical Civil Registry access is selective, not universal. MAGENHA does not undertake research involving recent persons or recent personal records; no universal age threshold defines eligibility. We no longer offer research involving records from Havana’s Cementerio de Colón.

For new and returning clients

Paid registration begins August 14, 2026.

Accepted new clients complete a paid one-time registration. Clients returning after a long period of inactivity are evaluated individually.

Before beginning registration, we conduct a brief feasibility review of the location, period, and type of research. If we cannot accept the request, the registration process does not begin.

If the request is accepted, we explain any applicable registration amount and the detailed conditions privately, before formal case planning or coordination starts. Registration is separate from later charges for records or other research.

Registration is not a membership or subscription. Clients with an ongoing or recent relationship do not repeat it. When a former client returns after an extended period of inactivity, we review the case individually and explain privately whether current registration or reactivation conditions apply before work resumes.

Research process

What happens after planning.

Once the research plan has been prepared, authorized local collaborators may consult historical Catholic parish and diocesan registers or historical Civil Registry records where access is available. These sources may be difficult to access, incompletely indexed or unavailable online. The sources and locality must be confirmed for each assignment.

Approximately three weeks is a typical reference, not a guarantee. Actual duration depends on archive availability and accessibility, collaborator schedules, the location and preservation of records, family research complexity and whether alternative parishes or archives must be consulted. While a case remains active, we aim to communicate every three or four weeks, or sooner if substantial progress occurs.

A missing record does not establish that the historical event never occurred. We may reassess the evidence and suggest other avenues, but new research directions are discussed with the client before proceeding. There is no automatic continuation, unlimited additional searching or indefinite continuous research.

i

Time estimates are not guarantees. Archive access, the survival and organization of records, and collaborator availability can all affect a search.

Records & contributions

What you receive and what it costs.

Our collaborators consult the historical records directly and are only authorized to take handwritten notes using paper and pencil. From these notes, our team prepares careful genealogical transcriptions containing the available documentary information and relevant archival references. For historical Civil Registry research, the output may be a detailed extract rather than a complete literal transcription, depending on how the record can be consulted.

Original register books cannot be photographed in any of the archives where MAGENHA currently conducts these consultations. Clients do not receive photographs or scans of original register-book pages. The original entry remains in the archive; MAGENHA’s transcription is a research document prepared from handwritten notes, not the original entry or a separately issued ecclesiastical certificate. An ecclesiastical certificate is a distinct document issued by the parish itself.

Sometimes MAGENHA Cuba may arrange for a parish to issue its own certificate, if the parish agrees and the request falls within our accepted services. This is an additional, separately agreed procedure; availability is not guaranteed and extra costs and delays may apply. We send only a digital copy of that certificate by email. This copy is not a photograph of an original historical register book.

Under no circumstances does MAGENHA ship physical documents outside Cuba. We do not arrange, coordinate or facilitate their physical export or international transport, nor handle legalization, apostilles or official document-delivery procedures.

Y-DNA & mtDNA

Lineage Research Program.

Voluntary · documentary · case by case

Clients who already have—or independently obtain—a relevant Y-DNA or mtDNA result may ask MAGENHA to evaluate the corresponding direct paternal or maternal lineage.

The tested person does not have to be the client. It may be an appropriate relative who represents the relevant direct paternal line for Y-DNA or direct maternal line for mtDNA.

01

What the program may support

If a lineage falls within the places and periods MAGENHA can research, the project may cover up to 100% of the standard genealogical research needed to document that specific uniparental line.

02

How eligibility is decided

Geographic, chronological, archival, and research capacity are evaluated individually. A DNA result does not by itself guarantee acceptance or a particular level of support.

Testing remains entirely in your hands

DNA testing is completely voluntary and is never required to use MAGENHA’s normal genealogy services. The client independently chooses and buys the test, submits the sample, manages the testing account, and receives the results. MAGENHA does not collect biological samples or operate a testing laboratory.

What MAGENHA does not request

  • Login credentials or passwords
  • Access to FamilyTreeDNA or other testing accounts
  • Raw DNA files or match lists
  • Broader private genetic information

For a program evaluation, it is normally enough to know the reported Y-DNA or mtDNA haplogroup, who was tested, and how that person connects genealogically to the relevant lineage.

The documentary principle

A haplogroup does not replace genealogy.

The program does not exist to sell DNA tests. Its purpose is to preserve direct paternal and maternal lineages before opportunities to test them disappear and to connect haplogroups with well-documented historical genealogies: ancestors, dates, places, migrations, parishes, municipalities, and a defensible chain of descent.

Important distinction

Genealogical research and legal purposes.

MAGENHA Cuba provides historical and genealogical research. It does not provide legal, citizenship, immigration or administrative document-procurement services, nor legal advice.

Some historical information we locate may be useful to clients pursuing such matters, but we cannot guarantee that a transcription, record, or research result will satisfy the evidentiary requirements of a government authority, consulate, court, church authority, or other institution.

If you require a document for legal or administrative purposes, independently confirm the type of certificate or official copy required by the relevant authority before proceeding.

Practical answers

Frequently asked questions

What records can MAGENHA research in Cuba?

Historical Catholic baptism, marriage and burial records, selectively historical Civil Registry records, and documentary evidence of family relationships. Access depends on authorized local collaborators and the archive. We no longer research Havana’s Cementerio de Colón records.

Can you research historical Civil Registry records?

Sometimes, where authorized access and local collaborators are available. Coverage is selective, not nationwide or guaranteed. Depending on the consultation conditions, we may provide a detailed extract rather than a complete literal transcription.

Do you research records concerning recent persons?

No. We do not undertake research involving recent persons or recent personal records. We assess the historical scope and access individually, without a universal age threshold.

Can I receive a photograph of the original parish register?

No. Original register books cannot be photographed in any archive where we currently conduct these consultations. Collaborators may only take handwritten notes using paper and pencil. We do not deliver photographs or scans of register-book pages.

What will I receive when a document is found?

A carefully prepared genealogical transcription based on handwritten consultation notes, with available documentary information and relevant archival references. Historical Civil Registry research may instead produce a detailed extract. The original entry remains in the archive.

Is a MAGENHA transcription an official certificate?

No. It is a research document prepared by our team, distinct from the original register entry and from a certificate separately issued by a parish.

Can you obtain a certificate issued by a Catholic parish?

Sometimes, if the parish agrees and the request falls within our accepted services. This is an additional, separately agreed procedure, with possible extra costs and delays. Availability is not guaranteed. We send only a digital copy by email; this is not a photograph of the original register book.

Can you send physical documents outside Cuba?

No, under any circumstances. MAGENHA does not arrange, coordinate or facilitate physical export or international transport of documents from Cuba. We do not handle legalization, apostilles or official document-delivery procedures. Any agreed parish certificate is delivered only as a digital copy by email.

How much does genealogical research cost?

US$5–30 per document is an approximate reference, not a binding quotation or a price for an entire family investigation. Not every document is available within that range. The amount depends on the record and research circumstances and is communicated individually. Registration and separately agreed extraordinary procedures or expenses are distinct.

Do I pay if the requested document cannot be found?

No normal success-based document contribution is charged if the requested document is not found. An applicable registration contribution is separate and is not automatically refundable simply because research is unsuccessful. Extraordinary expenses or different research arrangements must be communicated and accepted in advance.

Is there a separate research fee or an hourly charge?

We generally work with contributions associated with successfully located documents, rather than separate hourly research charges. Applicable registration, extraordinary procedures and any different arrangements are explained separately and agreed before work begins.

Do new clients need to register and pay an initial contribution?

Accepted new clients complete the one-time paid registration effective August 14, 2026, after the brief feasibility review. The amount and conditions are communicated privately. Active or recent clients do not repeat registration; clients returning after prolonged inactivity are assessed individually. Registration does not include later document contributions.

How long does a genealogical search usually take?

Approximately three weeks is a typical reference, not a guarantee. Archive availability and access, collaborator schedules, record location and preservation, family complexity and the need to consult alternative parishes or archives can change the duration.

What if the search takes longer than expected?

While a case remains active, we aim to update you every three or four weeks, or sooner for substantial progress. We can discuss obstacles and possible alternatives. There is no guaranteed completion date or indefinite continuous research; new directions are discussed before proceeding. A missing record does not prove the event never occurred.

What should I provide in my first inquiry?

Your name, email, country of residence, client status, purpose, research type, locality, approximate period and a brief goal with known historical facts. Existing archival references are helpful. Do not send identity documents, sensitive information about living people, private family documentation, DNA files or passwords at this stage.

What if I do not know the exact date, parish or spelling?

Give approximate dates, possible places and name variants, and distinguish confirmed information from assumptions. We assess whether there is a reasonable starting point; missing details may make a search harder or prevent acceptance.

Can you research several members of the same family?

Yes, where coverage and access allow it. We agree the people and scope with you. Finding one record does not authorize unlimited additional searches.

Are discounts available for additional family records?

Additional family documents may be offered at a reduced contribution depending on the research and circumstances. There is no universal sibling discount or fixed family package; any reduction is communicated individually.

Can I request more research after my first results?

Yes. We discuss new objectives, feasibility and applicable contributions or expenses before proceeding. Further research is optional and does not start automatically when a record is found.

Can you obtain documents for citizenship or other legal procedures?

No. MAGENHA Cuba does not provide legal, citizenship, immigration or administrative document-procurement services. Historical research and an exceptionally agreed parish certificate do not constitute such a service. We offer no legal advice or guarantee of acceptance by an authority.

How can I pay, and when will I receive the results?

Current methods include Zelle, PayPal, Bizum where applicable, cryptocurrency and other methods by prior arrangement; availability is confirmed for your location. Once a document is located and prepared, we explain the result and applicable contribution. After full payment, completed transcriptions are normally delivered within 24 hours. This is a delivery reference, not the search duration or a guarantee for parish certificates.

About Project MAGENHA

Preserve, connect, and share.

Project MAGENHA exists to preserve genealogical knowledge and keep fragile lines of evidence connected across generations. We bring documentary genealogy into conversation with Y-DNA and mtDNA haplogroups and help protect direct paternal and maternal lineages before the opportunity to test them disappears.

Documentary genealogy services are an ancillary fundraising activity supporting MAGENHA’s scientific work in genealogy and population genetics, not the project’s primary scientific purpose.

Our public mission also promotes interest in genealogy, genetics, archaeology, population history, and related fields. Funds collected by the project are allocated exclusively through the United States or Spain and are not transferred to countries such as Cuba, Venezuela, or Nicaragua.
